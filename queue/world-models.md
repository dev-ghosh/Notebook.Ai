# World Models in AI

## TL;DR
**A world model is an AI that learns how the world works (physics, space, cause and effect) and builds an internal "simulation" it can use to predict what happens next.** Instead of only predicting the next *word*, it predicts the next *moment* in a world.

---

## Simple analogy
Imagine pushing a glass towards the edge of a table. Without touching it, you already know it will fall and shatter. That is your brain's **world model**: a mental simulation of how things behave.

Most chatbots learned from text, so they know *about* the world through words. World models aim to learn the world directly from **video, images, sensors, and actions**.

## Why they matter
- **Robots** can practise in a simulated world instead of breaking real equipment.
- **Self-driving cars** can imagine "what if the car ahead brakes suddenly?"
- **AI agents** can plan ahead by trying actions in their head first.
- **Games and film**: generate interactive worlds from a prompt.
- Many researchers see world models as an important step toward more general AI (AGI).

## How they work (very simplified)
1. **Watch:** the model learns from huge amounts of video and sensor data.
2. **Learn the rules:** it picks up patterns like gravity, motion, and objects staying where they are.
3. **Predict:** given the current scene and an action ("turn left", "push the box"), it predicts what the next scene looks like.
4. **Plan:** an agent can run many "what if" predictions before choosing the best action.

## Examples
| Example | Who | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Genie 3** (and Project Genie) | Google DeepMind | Turns a text prompt or image into an interactive 3D world you can move around in, in real time. It can keep the world consistent for minutes, and lets you change events with new prompts |
| **Genie 1 and 2** | Google DeepMind | Earlier versions that created playable worlds from images |
| **Cosmos** | NVIDIA | Open world models for training robots and self-driving systems |
| **JEPA-style models** | Yann LeCun's research (Meta, and later his own lab) | An approach where the model learns to predict what matters about a scene, instead of every pixel |
| **Oasis** | Decart | A real-time, Minecraft-like world generated by AI |

## World model vs. a normal chatbot (LLM)
| | Chatbot (LLM) | World model |
|---|---|---|
| Learns from | Mostly text | Video, images, sensors, actions |
| Predicts | The next word | The next state of a world |
| Strength | Language, reasoning in words | Physics, space, planning actions |
| Typical use | Writing, coding, Q&A | Robots, driving, simulation, games |

(They can also be combined. Future systems may use both.)

## Current limits
- Worlds can lose consistency if you explore long enough.
- Physics is not always accurate.
- They cannot yet perfectly recreate real places or act as exact "digital twins".
- Access is limited for some tools (for example Project Genie has been limited to certain paid plans and regions).

## Quick recap
- World model = AI with an internal simulation of how the world works.
- Learns from video and actions, not just text.
- Examples: Genie 3, NVIDIA Cosmos, JEPA-style research.
- Big uses: robots, self-driving, games, and agents that plan.
