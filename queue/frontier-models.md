# Frontier Models in AI

## TL;DR
**Frontier models are the most capable AI models available at any given time.** They are built by a small number of labs pushing the limits of what AI can do. "Frontier" is relative: today's frontier model becomes tomorrow's average model.

---

## What does "frontier" mean?
Think of a map. The *frontier* is the edge of the explored land. In AI, it is the edge of what is possible right now: the smartest, most general models, usually trained at enormous cost with huge amounts of data and computing power.

## What makes a model "frontier"?
- **Top performance** on hard tests (maths, coding, science questions, reasoning).
- **General purpose**: one model handles writing, coding, images, analysis, and more.
- **Tool use**: can use search, code execution, and other tools to complete tasks.
- **Very expensive to build**: only a handful of companies can train them.

> Model names change every few months. Always check the company's website for the latest list.

## Frontier model vs. foundation model vs. LLM
- **LLM (Large Language Model):** an AI trained mainly on text.
- **Foundation model:** a big general model that can be adapted to many tasks (text, image, audio, etc.).
- **Frontier model:** the most advanced foundation models right now.

Simple rule: *all frontier models are foundation models, but not all foundation models are frontier.*

## Why do people care?
1. **Capability:** they can do things older models could not.
2. **Safety and regulation:** the most powerful models carry the biggest risks, so governments and labs watch them closely.
3. **Choosing a tool:** there is no single "best" model. Some are better at coding, some at cost, some at long documents. Many people use more than one.

## Quick recap
- Frontier = the cutting edge of AI right now.
- Built by a few big labs: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, xAI, and others.
- The label changes over time as new models arrive.
